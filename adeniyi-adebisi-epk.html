Book overview

What the book is

Africa's Underdevelopment: The Nigerian Predicament — An Exposé argues that Nigeria’s underdevelopment is not accidental, nor merely the product of corruption or incompetence. It is the result of deliberate structural arrangements rooted in colonial policy, preserved by institutional architecture, and accelerated by internal political dynamics.

The book documents the pathways through which these arrangements operate and offers specific paths for constitutional, fiscal, and institutional reform. It examines the colonial formation of Nigeria, the role of jihad, the Fulani political trajectory, British indirect rule, the Fulani-British pact, and the structural entrenchment of underdevelopment across Nigeria and Africa.

The work was first published in 2024 by Harmony Publishing. That edition covers the Nigerian market only.

Positioning

This is a politically direct, historically argued non-fiction work grounded in archival material, official records, and the author’s own institutional experience.

It is positioned for readers, editors, and rights partners interested in political history, African governance, constitutional argument, postcolonial power, and national restructuring debates.

Author profile

Who the author is

Adeniyi A. Adebisi is an 87-year-old Nigerian author, former civil servant, management consultant, and public affairs commentator. He served in Nigeria’s Federal Civil Service in the early republic, held senior finance roles with British Caledonian Airways and British Petroleum, and ran a management consultancy for over four decades.

He was elected National Co-ordinator of the Shareholders’ Association of Nigeria and has served on statutory audit committees of publicly listed companies.

His book combines archival research, official records, and six decades of institutional experience to argue that Nigeria’s underdevelopment is the outcome of deliberate structural arrangements. He writes as a witness: measured, authoritative, and uncompromising.

From the Preface

At independence, Britain simply re-assessed and re-aligned its Indirect Rule method of governance. Under the method, the Fulani and their conquered tribes in the Northern parts of Nigeria were positioned to rule independent Nigeria on its behalf with the collaboration of some malleable Southerners.

Many would ponder whether there was a formal agreement between the British and the Fulani, either written or unwritten. Yes, there was indeed an agreement, typewritten or handwritten, known only to the topmost hierarchies of the Fulani and the British cabals incorporating British business interest bodies like the Shell Petroleum Development Company, the United Africa Company, and of course, the British government.

From Chapter 6

Although Britain had ostensibly granted independence to Nigeria, she was and still is in charge of all the critical aspects of Nigeria — whether politically, economically, or about major developmental issues — through their Fulani agents and associates.

The onus, therefore, is on the natives to see through the well-orchestrated hypocrisy and conspiracy against them by total strangers who are greedy, ruthless and unscrupulous. They have to unite to resist being enslaved in their fatherland.
