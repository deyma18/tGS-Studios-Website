Case Study 01 — Literary & Media Campaign

An 87-year-old Nigerian statesman.
Zero digital footprint. A global stage.

Adeniyi A. Adebisi is a retired senior statesman, academic, and policy voice — eight decades of documented intellectual work, a published analysis of Nigeria's underdevelopment, and no presence in the digital world that matched the weight of his contribution. tGS Creative was brought in to change that.

The Context

Decades of work. No infrastructure to carry it.

The author had a compelling body of intellectual work — rooted in decades of direct experience with Nigerian governance, economic policy, and social development. He also had a placement on a premium London podcast network, Ninety-One Productions, and the beginning of a real media presence. What he lacked was the professional infrastructure to convert that moment into lasting visibility: no agent, no proposal, no digital profile, no representation framework, and no strategy connecting the Lagos market to London and the United States.

The assignment was to build that infrastructure from scratch — not to manage a campaign that already existed, but to design and construct one where nothing had been before.

The Challenge

Three markets. One voice. No room for error.

The work required simultaneous thinking across three distinct contexts: the UK literary market, where non-fiction agents operate within specific submission conventions and expect a completed book proposal before serious conversation begins; the Nigerian intellectual and media space, where the author's credibility is already established but underpublicised; and the United States, where the diaspora market and commercial non-fiction publishers represent the highest-value long-term opportunity.

A single misstep in approach — a poorly timed submission, a query letter that misread an agent's positioning, a representation agreement that left either party exposed — could close doors that take years to reopen in this space.

The Approach

Intelligence first. Infrastructure second. Outreach third.

tGS Creative began with a comprehensive literary agent intelligence exercise — identifying and verifying ten agents across four categories: UK prestige houses, memoir and international voices specialists, history and current affairs specialists, and Africa-focused literary representation. Submission windows, individual agent positioning, and agency submission rules were all mapped before a single letter was written.

A three-wave submission strategy was designed: priority UK agents in Wave 1, the highest-value targets in Wave 2 following the opening of submission windows, and US commercial agents in Wave 3 once UK results were in hand. Five fully individualised query letter templates were produced — each one calibrated to the specific agent, their stated interests, and their preferred submission format.

Simultaneously, a representation agreement was drafted between tGS Creative Works and the author, covering commission structure, expense recoupment, advance splits, and rights architecture — bringing the relationship onto a proper commercial footing before any external submission was made.

The Infrastructure Built

A complete professional presence, built to last.

Alongside the literary campaign, tGS Creative built the author's digital and professional infrastructure from the ground up. A fully rewritten LinkedIn profile established his public identity with the authority his career warranted. A company page for tGS Creative Works was built and positioned to carry the campaign's credibility in both directions — demonstrating that the management behind the project was serious.

A non-fiction book proposal — the binding document that UK and US agents require before formal consideration — was scoped and structured, with chapter outline, market analysis framework, and comparative title research all mapped as the next phase of delivery.

The Ninety-One Productions podcast placement was positioned as the universal proof point across all query letters: concrete evidence of media traction rather than a promise of future visibility.

Infrastructure complete. Campaign staged and ready.

The representation agreement has been delivered to the author for review. The three-wave submission strategy is built, the agent roster is verified, and the query letters are written. Wave 1 outreach — to Isobel Dixon at Blake Friedmann, Elise Dillsworth, Veronique Baxter at David Higham, and Toby Mundy at Aevitas Creative Management — is ready to deploy the moment the representation agreement is signed and the book proposal is complete. Case Study 02 — Market Mindset Training Programme

Teaching people to think like market players.
Using a real product in a real market.

The MPS Beauty Marketing Cohort is a tGS Creative–designed and directed training programme. Its subject is market thinking. Its medium is social media. Its product is real: MPS Face Masks and Hand Creams, sold in the Lagos beauty market.

The Context

A real product. A real market. Beginners who need to learn to think, not just post.

MPS Retail operates in Lagos — one of Africa's largest and most competitive beauty markets. The business needed people who could run promotional campaigns effectively: on WhatsApp, on Instagram, and eventually in direct sales contexts. The problem was not a shortage of people willing to post. It was a shortage of people who understood why they were posting, who they were posting for, and what they expected to happen as a result.

The brief to tGS Creative was not "build us a social media team." It was something harder and more valuable: build us a cohort of marketers who understand the market, understand the buyer, and can act on that understanding with discipline and clarity.

The Design Principle

The platform is a tool. The mind is the asset.

tGS Creative designed the entire Level 1 curriculum around a single principle: think before you post. Every session, every task, every assessment criterion is downstream of one question — can this person explain why they are saying something, to whom, on which platform, and what they expect to happen?

A trainee who can answer that question fluently is a marketable asset. A trainee who cannot — regardless of how polished their content looks — is a content scheduler, not a marketer. The curriculum is designed to produce the former and identify the latter early, before they cost the brand credibility in the market.

The Structure

Three waves. One market. Real buyers from day one.

Wave 1 — Market Awareness. Trainees learn the Lagos beauty market as a system before they are allowed to touch a campaign brief. They map the five key market players — seller, buyer, product, competitor, regulator — in the specific context of MPS products. They understand NAFDAC compliance as a trust signal, not a legal obligation. By the end of Wave 1, each trainee can describe a real Lagos buyer with real motivations, real fears, and a real decision-making process.

Wave 2 — Guided Imitation. Trainees receive a complete campaign brief and a working example. They learn the five-question brief structure: audience, offer, message, channel, call to action. They produce their own version of the campaign in their own voice, receive specific critique, and revise based on feedback. The assessment is not whether the content is polished — it is whether the trainee can explain their choices under questioning.

Wave 3 — Simple Adaptation. Trainees move to a new brief, a new product, and a new audience without being given an example to copy. They produce an independent mini-campaign and — critically — they transfer the same marketing logic to an offline selling context. A verbal pitch to a salon owner. A product display card for a pop-up stall. If the logic holds offline, the training has produced a marketer. If it collapses, it has produced a content scheduler.

The Delivery Model

Remote direction. Real-time feedback. No heavy infrastructure.

tGS Creative directs the programme from the UK. Trainees operate in Lagos. All session content is delivered via WhatsApp voice notes, written briefs, and video calls — the same channels the trainees will use when they are running actual campaigns. The training environment and the working environment are deliberately identical.

Feedback is specific, not general. Not "this is good" — but "the price is buried on line four. Move it to the first sentence. Here is why that matters." Trainees learn to receive critique as market information, not personal judgement. This, too, is part of the training: the ability to improve under instruction is one of the five assessment criteria, and it is weighted equally with execution quality.

The programme is designed to run with minimal overhead. There is no classroom, no LMS platform, no dedicated app. The operating model is lean by design — because the most important tools in a Lagos beauty market are a working phone and a disciplined mind.

What This Demonstrates

tGS Creative builds programmes, not just plans.

The MPS Beauty Cohort is not a consultancy output — it is not a strategy deck or a positioning document. It is a live, running programme that is producing measurable change in real people's capacity to operate in a real market. The curriculum is designed, the assessment framework is built, Level 1 is in delivery, and the progression criteria for Level 2 are already defined.

This is what tGS Creative's management and content strategy services look like when they are applied to a structured training context: a coherent framework, a clear progression logic, and a delivery model that works across borders with minimal infrastructure. The same thinking that built this curriculum is available to any client who needs people, processes, or programmes built around a real market objective.

The Bigger Picture

A replicable model for any product in any market.

The curriculum tGS Creative designed for MPS is not product-specific in its logic. The five-question brief works for any product. The market mapping exercise works for any market. The three-wave progression — awareness, guided imitation, independent adaptation — is a training architecture that can be rebuilt around a cosmetics brand in Abuja, a fashion label in Accra, or a food vendor network in any West African city with a smartphone-dense consumer base.

tGS Creative owns this framework. Level 1 is documented. Levels 2 and 3 are the natural extension. The cohort model — remote direction, real product context, WhatsApp-first delivery, offline transfer as the final proof point — is the intellectual property of tGS Creative Works and a service that is available to licence, adapt, or deploy in partnership.

Current Status

Level 1 in delivery. Level 2 in design.

The Level 1 curriculum is complete and in active delivery with the first cohort. Wave 1 and Wave 2 frameworks are running. Wave 3 assessment criteria are set. The programme is on track for the first cohort to reach Level 2 progression criteria within the next four to six weeks. The Level 2 curriculum — which moves trainees from guided imitation to independent campaign planning across multiple platforms and audience segments — is currently being scoped by tGS Creative.
