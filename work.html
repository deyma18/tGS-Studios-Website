Case Study 01: Literary and Media Campaign

An 87-year-old Nigerian statesman.
No digital footprint. A global stage to build.

Adeniyi A. Adebisi is a retired senior statesman, academic, and policy voice with decades of documented intellectual work and a published analysis of Nigeria's underdevelopment. When tGS Creative came in, he had no digital presence as an author, no media profile, and no infrastructure to carry his work to the audiences that needed to find it. The assignment was to build all of that from nothing.

The Context

Decades of work. No infrastructure to carry it.

The author had a compelling body of intellectual work, rooted in direct experience with Nigerian governance, economic policy, and social development. What he did not have was any of the infrastructure that converts intellectual credibility into public presence and commercial opportunity: no author profile, no agent, no proposal, no digital footprint, no media placements, and no representation framework.

tGS Creative was not brought in to manage a campaign that already existed. The assignment was to design and build one where nothing had been before, and to do so across three markets simultaneously: the UK, Nigeria, and the United States.

The Challenge

Three markets. One voice. No room for error.

The UK literary market operates within specific submission conventions. Non-fiction agents expect a completed book proposal before serious conversation begins, and the wrong approach at the wrong time closes doors that take years to reopen. The Nigerian intellectual and media space offered established credibility but no published author profile to build from. The United States represented the highest-value long-term opportunity, through both the diaspora market and commercial non-fiction publishers.

Every decision, from which agent to approach first to how the representation agreement was structured, had to be made with all three markets in view at once.

The Approach

Intelligence first. Infrastructure second. Outreach third.

tGS Creative began with a comprehensive literary agent intelligence exercise: identifying and verifying ten agents across four categories, covering UK prestige houses, memoir and international voices specialists, history and current affairs specialists, and Africa-focused literary representation. Submission windows, individual agent positioning, and agency submission rules were all mapped before a single letter was written.

A three-wave submission strategy followed. Priority UK agents in Wave 1, the highest-value targets in Wave 2 after submission windows opened, and US commercial agents in Wave 3 once UK results were in hand. Five fully individualised query letter templates were produced, each calibrated to the specific agent, their stated interests, and their preferred submission format.

A representation agreement was drafted between tGS Creative Works and the author in parallel, covering commission structure, expense recoupment, advance splits, and rights architecture, bringing the relationship onto a proper commercial footing before any external submission was made.

The Infrastructure Built

A complete author presence, created from scratch.

tGS Creative built the author's entire digital and professional presence from the ground up. A fully written LinkedIn profile established his public identity as an author and policy voice, with the authority his career warranted. A placement on a premium London podcast network was secured, giving the campaign a concrete media proof point to reference in outreach. A company page for tGS Creative Works was built and positioned alongside the author's profile, demonstrating that the management behind the project was credible and operational.

A non-fiction book proposal, the document UK and US agents require before formal consideration, was scoped and structured: chapter outline, market analysis framework, and comparative title research all mapped as the next phase of delivery. The podcast placement was positioned as the universal proof point across all query letters, concrete evidence of media traction rather than a promise of future visibility.

Current Status

Infrastructure complete. Campaign staged and ready.

The representation agreement has been delivered to the author for review. The three-wave submission strategy is built, the agent roster is verified, and the query letters are written. Wave 1 outreach to Isobel Dixon at Blake Friedmann, Elise Dillsworth, Veronique Baxter at David Higham, and Toby Mundy at Aevitas Creative Management is ready to deploy the moment the representation agreement is signed and the book proposal is complete. The campaign is not waiting for an idea. It is waiting for a signature.

Case Study 02: Market Mindset Training Programme

Teaching people to think like market players.
Using a real product in a real market.

The MPS Beauty Marketing Cohort is a tGS Creative-designed and directed training programme. Its subject is market thinking. Its medium is social media. Its product is real: MPS Face Masks and Hand Creams, sold across the Nigerian beauty market.

The Context

A real product. A real market. People who need to learn to think, not just post.

MPS Retail operates in Nigeria, one of Africa's largest and most competitive beauty markets. The business needed people who could run promotional campaigns effectively: on WhatsApp, on Instagram, and eventually in direct sales contexts. The problem was not a shortage of people willing to post. It was a shortage of people who understood why they were posting, who they were posting for, and what they expected to happen as a result.

The brief to tGS Creative was not "build us a social media team." It was something harder and more valuable: build a cohort of marketers who understand the market, understand the buyer, and can act on that understanding with discipline and clarity.

The Design Principle

The platform is a tool. The mind is the asset.

tGS Creative designed the entire Level 1 curriculum around a single principle: think before you post. Every session, every task, and every assessment criterion is downstream of one question: can this person explain why they are saying something, to whom, on which platform, and what they expect to happen?

A trainee who can answer that question fluently is a marketable asset. A trainee who cannot, regardless of how polished their content looks, is a content scheduler rather than a marketer. The curriculum is designed to produce the former and identify the latter early, before they cost the brand credibility in the market.

The Structure

Three waves. One market. Real buyers from day one.

Wave 1: Market Awareness. Trainees learn the Nigerian beauty market as a system before they are allowed to touch a campaign brief. They map the five key market players, namely seller, buyer, product, competitor, and regulator, in the specific context of MPS products. They understand NAFDAC compliance as a trust signal, not merely a legal obligation. By the end of Wave 1, each trainee can describe a real buyer with real motivations, real fears, and a real decision-making process.

Wave 2: Guided Imitation. Trainees receive a complete campaign brief and a working example. They learn the five-question brief structure: audience, offer, message, channel, and call to action. They produce their own version of the campaign in their own voice, receive specific critique, and revise based on feedback. The assessment is not whether the content is polished; it is whether the trainee can explain their choices under questioning.

Wave 3: Simple Adaptation. Trainees move to a new brief, a new product, and a new audience without being given an example to copy. They produce an independent mini-campaign and, critically, they transfer the same marketing logic to an offline selling context: a verbal pitch to a salon owner, or a product display card for a pop-up stall. If the logic holds offline, the training has produced a marketer. If it collapses without the platform, it has produced a content scheduler.

The Delivery Model

Remote direction. Real-time feedback. No heavy infrastructure.

tGS Creative directs the programme from the UK. Trainees operate remotely from across West Africa, and the programme is open to participants wherever they are based. All session content is delivered through the same channels trainees will use in their actual work: WhatsApp voice notes, written briefs, and video calls. The training environment and the working environment are deliberately identical.

Feedback is specific, not general. Not "this is good," but "the price is buried on line four. Move it to the first sentence. Here is why that matters." Trainees learn to receive critique as market information, not personal judgement. This is part of the training: the ability to improve under instruction is one of the five assessment criteria, weighted equally with execution quality.

The programme is designed to run with minimal overhead. There is no classroom, no LMS platform, and no dedicated app. The operating model is lean by design, because the most important tools in any market are a working phone and a disciplined mind.

What This Demonstrates

tGS Creative builds programmes, not just plans.

The MPS Beauty Cohort is not a conventional consultancy output. It is not a strategy deck or a positioning document. It is a live, running programme producing measurable change in real people's capacity to operate in a real market. The curriculum is designed, the assessment framework is built, Level 1 is in delivery, and the progression criteria for Levels 2 and 3 are defined and in design.

This is what tGS Creative's management and content strategy services look like when applied to a structured training context: a coherent framework, a clear progression logic, and a delivery model that works across borders with minimal infrastructure. The same thinking that built this curriculum is available to any client who needs people, processes, or programmes built around a real market objective.

The Bigger Picture

A replicable model for any product in any market.

The curriculum tGS Creative designed for MPS is not product-specific in its logic. The five-question brief works for any product. The market mapping exercise works for any market. The three-wave progression, from awareness through guided imitation to independent adaptation, is a training architecture that can be rebuilt around a cosmetics brand, a fashion label, or a vendor network in any market with a smartphone-dense consumer base.

tGS Creative owns this framework. Level 1 is fully documented and in active delivery. Levels 2 and 3 are designed and being built out. The cohort model, built on remote direction, real product context, mobile-first delivery, and offline transfer as the final proof point, is the intellectual property of tGS Creative Works and is available to licence, adapt, or deploy in partnership.

Current Status

Level 1 in delivery. Levels 2 and 3 in design.

The Level 1 curriculum is complete and in active delivery with the first cohort. Wave 1 and Wave 2 frameworks are running. Wave 3 assessment criteria are set. The programme is on track for the first cohort to reach Level 2 progression criteria within the coming weeks. Levels 2 and 3, which move trainees from guided imitation to independent campaign planning across multiple platforms, audience segments, and market conditions, are currently being designed by tGS Creative. New participants are welcome: the programme is open to trainees regardless of where they are based.
